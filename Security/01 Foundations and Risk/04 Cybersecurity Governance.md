# Cybersecurity Governance

### Concept and Scope of Information Security

Information Security is the discipline that defines standards, procedures, controls, and tools designed to safeguard strategic data and intangible assets of organizations against theft, unauthorized access, illegitimate modifications, unavailability, or destruction. This discipline applies transversally to all corporate sectors that handle sensitive data, such as human resources, finance, or sales departments.

Information security governance is based on four structural elements:

1. **Policies:** Formal and documented guidelines that establish legal permissions and prohibitions within the corporate environment (for example, restricting the copying of confidential data to removable storage devices, such as pen drives). Violating these rules implies administrative and disciplinary sanctions.
2. **Mechanisms:** Technical tools of a logical or physical nature used to manage security, including antivirus solutions, firewall systems, and closed-circuit television (CCTV) surveillance.
3. **Culture:** The level of security maturity within the organization. Institutions with high maturity levels promote continuous training, lectures, and onboarding processes for new employees, aligning them with current standards.
4. **Conduct and Mindset:** Conduct reflects the practical application of security principles in daily life (such as discretion when sharing civil identification data in commercial establishments). A security *mindset* adopts an analytical perspective similar to that of an auditor or ethical hacker, focusing on anticipating flaws and proactivity mitigating vulnerabilities prior to their potential exploitation.
## Cyber Threat Management

Cyber threat management is a framework used to manage the entire lifecycle of a threat, enabling rapid and appropriate identification and response. It relies on the seamless integration of **people, processes, and technology** to stay ahead of adversaries.

- **Early Detection & Response:** Spotting malicious activity early is critical to neutralizing threats before they cause widespread damage.
- **The Role of AI and Automation:** Advanced tools, specifically Automation and Artificial Intelligence (AI), act as force multipliers. They allow security teams to analyze evolving threat landscapes and data at machine speed.
- **Business Impact:** Effective management prevents financial loss (theft, ransomware, fines) and preserves the organization’s reputation and customer trust.
## The NIST Cybersecurity Framework

The NIST framework provides standards to help organizations improve their methods for combating threats by organizing the threat lifecycle into five core functions:

- **Identify:** Understanding the business context, systems, assets, and data that need protection to manage risk effectively.    
- **Protect:** Implementing safeguards to ensure the delivery of critical services and contain the impact of potential incidents.
- **Detect:** Defining the protocols and activities needed to promptly identify the occurrence of a cybersecurity event.
- **Respond:** Executing specific actions to contain the impact once an incident is detected.
- **Recover:** Restoring impaired capabilities or services to maintain business continuity and initiate disaster recovery.
## Operational Excellence for Developers

For those building and maintaining systems, governance translates into clear operational procedures:

- **Incident Management:** Focuses on returning services to "normal operation" as quickly as possible to minimize business impact.
- **Problem Management:** A continuous process involving developers to detect and eliminate the **root causes** of incidents, rather than just treating symptoms.
- **Service Continuity:** Ensures that services can be resumed after a crisis through predefined guidance and disaster recovery strategies.
## **Strategic Frameworks and Standards**

- **ITSM & ITIL:** Focus on delivering IT as a service, integrating the entire service lifecycle with business needs.
- **COBIT:** Helps align technical issues and control requirements with business risks.
- **ISO 27001:** A comprehensive standard for the implementation and management of information security systems.
- **ISO 27014:** Provides specific guidance on the concepts and processes for governing information security.
