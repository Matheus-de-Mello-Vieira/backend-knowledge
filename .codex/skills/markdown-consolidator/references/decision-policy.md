# Lossless decision policy

## Core rule

Decisions control presentation and status, not survival. Every readable, in-scope source unit must remain represented in the final document or an appendix.

## Source authority

Use this default order, adjusted for the domain:

1. Current primary specification or official documentation.
2. Current official release notes or maintained source code.
3. Approved internal documentation with a named owner and date.
4. Reputable technical publication with direct evidence.
5. Community discussion or personal notes.
6. Unsourced statements.

Authority affects labeling and ordering. It does not authorize deletion.

## Resolution sequence

1. Confirm entity and operation.
2. Compare version, date, platform, environment, configuration, role, locale, and mode.
3. Check general rule versus exception.
4. Check publication dates and maintenance status.
5. Prefer primary evidence for the current recommendation.
6. Preserve all other evidence as alternatives, exceptions, historical behavior, or unresolved positions.
7. Record evidence, confidence, residual uncertainty, and final representation for every unit.

## Equivalent information

Units may be merged only when one final statement can express their complete semantic union. Every contributing unit must map to that final statement. Preserve distinct examples, caveats, rationales, and qualifiers even when the central claim is equivalent.

## Outdated information

Mark information historical or superseded only with evidence. Retain it in a historical/deprecated subsection, migration note, comparison table, or appendix. State when and where it applied.

## Unresolved conflicts

Preserve all relevant claims, their scopes, their evidence, and the missing fact needed to resolve the conflict. Never average incompatible values or create a false compromise.

## Material outside the main narrative

Move it to an appendix titled `Additional Details`, `Historical Information`, `Contextual Variants`, or another precise heading. Do not drop it.

## Publication attribution boundary

Source authority and evidence are internal decision inputs. Do not expose local filenames, paths, source IDs, unit IDs, or synthetic labels such as `Source A` in the final publication.

Describe accepted, alternative, historical, and unresolved positions by substantive scope: version, date, platform, environment, configuration, role, operating mode, or the content of the claim. Preserve external references that are part of the source content, but never create references to the temporary input files.
